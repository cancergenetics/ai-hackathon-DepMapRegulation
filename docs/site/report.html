DepMapTR · method explainer

Copy-number trans effects in cancer cell lines

In one sentence: DepMapTR asks which genes change expression when another gene's copy number changes; it takes matched copy-number and RNA-seq profiles of 1,436 DepMap cancer cell lines, scores all 210 million pairs of genes on different chromosomes for how well the copy number of a regulator gene A predicts the mRNA of a target gene B once confounders are removed, and outputs a ranked list of regulator→target pairs with a predicted direction of change.

The method in three sentences

  1. Natural copy-number variation across cell lines is treated as a weak, confounded experiment: B's mRNA is regressed on A's copy number, adjusting for B's own copy number, cell-line covariates (lineage, ploidy, culture medium and others) and the copy number of every chromosome arm outside A's chromosome.
  2. Only regulators whose own mRNA clearly rises with their copy number are kept (the cis gate), and each signal is shared among genes that are always gained or lost together with A, favouring the one whose mRNA tracks its dosage best (attribution).
  3. Each pair's score is the adjusted t-statistic × the gate (1 or 0) × the attribution share; pairs are ranked by its size, and the opposite of its sign is the predicted change in B when A is knocked down (Step 4).

What it showed. On held-out regulators the top 0.1% of ranked pairs were 3.85 times richer in CRISPRi Perturb-seq hits than the rest, but this is not significant under the pre-registered test (p = 0.068) and not significantly better than the same method run on shuffled copy number. The one replicated result, that the cis gate helps (+0.97 log-OR, 95% CI 0.30–1.64), suggests copy number says something about which regulators have detectable downstream effects; no information about which targets respond was detected, and the predicted direction is not established (Results).

How it differs from standard approaches

The usual ways of finding trans effects either mistake co-gained chromosome arms for regulation, or remove genuine dosage signal together with the noise. This method keeps copy number as the upstream "cause" and adjusts only for confounders that are not downstream of A: cell-line covariates and the measured copy number of other chromosome arms.

Why it matters. A copy-number association is only useful as a prediction of "what happens if this gene's dosage changes" if it survives adjustment for the things that co-vary with dosage. The two extra ingredients, the cis gate and attribution, also borrow from genetics: the cis gate is the "strong instrument" condition of Mendelian randomisation [G2, B2], and attribution is single-effect fine-mapping [W4].

Step 1 · Association with other chromosomes' arms adjusted

What
For every regulator A and every target B on another chromosome, a linear regression across lines of B's mRNA on A's copy number, adjusting for B's own copy number, the covariates, and the arm-level copy number of every arm not on A's chromosome. The t-statistic is the evidence.
Why
Cancers gain and lose arms in combinations, so A's copy number is correlated with distant arms. Adjusting for them removes that. A's own chromosome is left out of the adjustment because A's arm carries A's own signal.
Without it
Lineage and co-gained arms create false targets: GREB1 goes from t = 6.1 (naive) to 1.5 (full model).
Came from
TP1 (chosen from the literature); TP6 (three alternatives failed to beat it). Read Step 1

Step 2 · Cis gate

What
Keeps regulator A only if its own mRNA rises strongly with its own copy number (adjusted t ≥ 10). Regulators that fail get a score of 0 for every target. 5,403 of 12,141 regulators pass.
Why
If more copies of A do not mean more A mRNA, then A's copy number does not perturb A, and any trans association belongs to its neighbours or its arm.
Without it
Held-out OR drops from 3.85 to 1.46. This is the one gain in the project that replicated on held-out data (+0.97 log-OR, 95% CI 0.30–1.64).
Came from
TP4 (specified), TP6 (adopted), TP8 (replicated). Read Step 2

Step 3 · Co-segment attribution

What
Groups neighbouring genes whose copy numbers move together (r ≥ 0.95) into blocks, then, separately for each target, splits the block's signal among its genes, favouring the gene whose own mRNA tracks its copy number best.
Why
Genes in the same amplicon are statistically indistinguishable in trans. Without a tie-breaker every passenger gene inherits the driver's score.
Without it
In the worked example, PIK3C2B (2 hits in the screen) would score like its neighbour PPP1R15B (835 hits).
Came from
TP4 (specified), TP6 (adopted, +0.36 log-OR on training data), TP7 (shown to act through regulator choice). Read Step 3

What

Why
One number per pair is needed for ranking. CRISPRi knockdown resembles copy loss, so the direction flips.
Status
Direction not established The test of direction also "passes" for a negative control built from shuffled copy number.

TP4, TP8. Read Step 4

Re-weights the copy-number score by how highly target B is expressed in one cell line (here HCT116, taken from DepMap), so pairs that a screen in that line can detect rise to the top.

The steps in plain language

Each step exists because something specific went wrong without it. The numbers below are from the final, selected configuration.

  1. 1

    Measure the A→B association with confounders removed

    For each regulator A and each target B on a different chromosome, the method fits one linear regression across the 1,436 lines: B's mRNA on A's copy number, adjusting for B's own copy number (plus a flag for complete loss of B where at least 3 lines have one), the 56 covariate columns, a measure of overall aneuploidy, and the arm-level copy number of every chromosome arm not on A's chromosome. This adjustment is called leave-own-chromosome-out (LOCO).

    y_B = β·x_A + γ·[x_B, deletion_B] + Σ_(arms k not on chr(A)) δ_k·arm_k + covariates + error
    Why
    Cancers gain and lose whole arms in recurring combinations (for example, 88 co-occurring arm pairs across tumours [T1, S4]). Without adjustment, A's copy number stands in for those other arms. A's own chromosome is excluded from the adjustment because adjusting for A's own arm would remove most of A's own variation.
    Without it
    GREB1 appears to respond to PPP1R15B with raw t = 6.1. Lineage and covariates bring it to 2.7, and other arms' copy number to 1.5. Expression factors are deliberately not used (see above).
    Evidence
    Three alternatives (also adjusting A's own arm, leaving out only A's arm, or using copy-number principal components) did not beat this choice in training. On its own, though, Step 1 is weak: training OR 1.59, permutation p 0.25.
    Speed
    The regression is solved in closed form (Frisch–Waugh–Lovell: residualise everything on the covariates once per chromosome, then compute partial correlations by matrix products), matching a standard regression package to 1e-8.

    Produced at TP1; confirmed at TP6.

  2. 2

    Keep only regulators whose own mRNA follows their copy number

    For each regulator, the method measures how strongly its own mRNA rises with its own copy number, with the same adjustment. A passes the cis gate if this adjusted t is at least 10 (equivalently, a first-stage F-statistic of at least 100, the usual measure of instrument strength). Regulators that fail score 0 for every target. 5,403 of 12,141 regulators pass.

    Why
    Copy number can only perturb A's targets through A if it changes A's product. This is the Mendelian-randomisation logic: copy number is an "instrument" for A's expression, and weak instruments give biased, noisy answers [G2, B2].
    Without it
    Held-out OR falls from 3.85 to 1.46. The paired difference, +0.97 log-OR (95% CI 0.30–1.64), is the only gain that replicated from training (+0.79) to held-out data.
    What it does not do
    It chooses regulators; it does not reorder a regulator's targets (target-level statistic changed by −0.05). It also measures dosage response across all lineages, not in the screened line: MYCN passes through neuroblastoma amplicons but is not expressed in HCT116, and on training arms it took 1,701 top-ranked slots for 1 hit.

    Specified at TP4, adopted at TP6, replicated at TP8.

  3. 3

    Share each signal among genes that are gained and lost together

    Along each chromosome, neighbouring genes whose adjusted copy numbers correlate at r ≥ 0.95 with the running block average are grouped into blocks (co-segments; 5,126 genome-wide). For each target B the method assumes exactly one gene in the block drives B, and computes each gene's posterior share α. This is single-effect fine-mapping (SuSiE with one effect [W4]), with an approximate Bayes factor computed from each gene's t with B (Wakefield's closed form; no citation in the project's literature file).

    α(g | B) ∝ prior(g) × BF(g, B) prior(g) ∝ exp(t_cis,g / 2) prior sd of effect σ₀ = 0.05
    Why
    Within an amplicon the trans t-statistics are nearly identical (in the example, 7.0–7.5 for four genes), so the data alone cannot pick the driver. Without a tie-breaker every passenger gets the driver's score and fills the top of the list.
    In practice
    Because the data barely discriminate, the prior decides: α behaves almost like "give the block to the gene with the strongest cis response". Its training gain (+0.36 log-OR; lower bound +0.04, barely above zero) came through choosing regulators, not targets.
    Not validated
    Attribution was not separately ablated on the held-out arms, so its held-out value is unknown.

    Specified at TP4, adopted at TP6, diagnosed at TP7.

  4. 4

    Score, rank and predict a direction

    The score is S = t × gate weight (1 or 0) × α. All trans pairs are ranked by |S|. The predicted change of B when A is knocked down is the opposite of the sign of S, flipped again for the 3 regulators whose mRNA falls with more copies (cis t ≤ −3).

    Why
    Ranking needs one number per pair. CRISPRi knockdown lowers A, which resembles copy loss, so "more A copies, more B" predicts "knock A down, B goes down".
    Status
    Direction not established. 71.8% of the 131 top-ranked held-out hits had the predicted direction, against 64.7% expected under a within-regulator shuffle (p = 0.012). But a negative control built from shuffled copy number also passes the same test (p = 0.001), so the test is miscalibrated (Results).
    Calls
    A thresholded call set (|t| ≥ 3.016 at 5% permutation FDR) has 10.7 million trans pairs and held-out OR 1.15. Ranking is the recommended use.

    Specified at TP4; evidential status set at TP8.

  5. 5

    Optional: re-rank for what one cell line can detect

    The context layer multiplies the copy-number score's percentile by the square of target B's expression percentile in one line (HCT116, from DepMap's own profile, no benchmark labels): S_ctx = sign(t) · ρ_P · ρ_T². It is reported next to the main method, not as part of it.

    Why
    Perturb-seq detects changes mainly in highly expressed genes; within a regulator, target expression in HCT116 alone ranks hits with AUROC about 0.70 (training).
    What it adds
    Held-out OR 18.4 (95% CI 10.9–30.9; permutation p 0.015). It beats the same fusion built on shuffled copy number (+0.55 log-OR, CI 0.07–1.03), which suggests real copy-number evidence helps choose regulators (a post-hoc comparison, not multiplicity-corrected). It is not different from a label-free "hub" baseline (OR 22.8), and shuffling targets within each regulator barely changes it (17.3).
    Use with care
    It ranks by detectability, not by effect size. It was added after seeing training results.

    Produced at TP7.

  6. E

    Evaluation: one sealed test on held-out chromosome arms

    The benchmark universe is every trans pair whose regulator was knocked down in the HCT116 screen and has variable copy number in DepMap, and whose target is measured in both. Regulators were split by whole chromosome arm into 28 training and 14 test arms (1,817 test regulators, 17.7 million test pairs). Test labels were sealed before any tuning and opened once in a locked run.

    Metric
    Odds ratio of Perturb-seq hits (adjusted p < 0.05) among the top 0.1% of pairs by |S|.
    Significance
    Regulator labels are permuted (1,000 times) because hits cluster by regulator: the top 1% of perturbations give 55% of HCT116 trans hits. Confidence intervals come from deleting one test arm at a time (jackknife).
    Baselines
    Random, naive correlation, co-expression, target expression variability, target expression in HCT116, a "hub prior" that combines regulator cis strength with target expression, and arm copy number alone.
    Controls
    Copy number shuffled within lineage (should score ~1); targets shuffled within each regulator (tests whether the method ranks targets); the same within-regulator view as a stratified odds ratio (CMH) and per-regulator AUROC.

    Shaped at TP2, TP3, TP5, TP7, TP8.

Worked example: PPP1R15B on chromosome 1q

One real regulator, traced through every step with the project's own code. PPP1R15B (also called CReP) directs protein phosphatase 1 to eIF2α; losing it switches on the integrated stress response. It sits in a co-amplified block at 1q32.1 that also contains the oncogene MDM4 (marked on the chromosome at the top of the page). It is a training regulator, so only training labels are used and held-out labels stay sealed. Every number is read from worked_example.json, embedded in this page.

1 · How much does PPP1R15B's copy number vary?

Across 1,436 lines, the median line carries 18% more copies of PPP1R15B than its ploidy (relative CN 1.18), because 1q is often gained. lines have at least a 2× gain and has a 2× loss, comfortably above the 20 lines with a 2-fold change required to score a regulator. The extreme lines are focal amplicons in brain and soft-tissue tumours (relative CN up to 38.7), clipped at 8× so they cannot dominate. HCT116 itself sits near neutral (1.10).

2 · Does its own mRNA follow its copy number? (cis gate)

Adjusted for the 98 design columns, PPP1R15B's mRNA rises with a slope of log2 units per doubling: roughly proportional dosage. The adjusted t is (F = ), far above the gate at 10, so its weight is 1. Its block neighbours are shown below: LRRN2 (t = 8.5) fails the gate and scores 0 everywhere.

3 · What does adjustment do to its trans associations?

Three targets on other chromosomes, refitted one at a time (the fast engine matched to within 1e-14). MYBL2 and NT5E keep strong associations. GREB1 is the warning case: of all testable targets with adjusted |t| < 2, it had the strongest raw correlation. A plausible reading is that 1q gain is common in the lineages (for example breast) that express this oestrogen target, and 1q is co-gained with other arms. That is what the arm adjustment removes.

Across all 16,388 trans targets, PPP1R15B's |t| has median 1.35, about twice what pure noise would give (≈ 0.67). Much of that is a signature shared by the whole 1q region, which is why Step 3 exists.

4 · Who in the co-amplified block gets the credit?

Chromosome 1 has 1,938 genes with copy-number data, grouped into 483 blocks. PPP1R15B's block holds 4 genes within 0.3 Mb. For the target MYBL2, all four genes have nearly the same t (left), so the trans data cannot pick a driver; the prior from each gene's cis strength decides (right).

One anecdote, not proof. PIK3C2B passes the gate and was itself knocked down in the screen, yet gets α ≈ 0.002 (median over its targets; 0.0002 for MYBL2); in the screen it produced 2 trans hits against PPP1R15B's 835, so here the credit went to the right gene. MDM4 keeps about 14% (median over targets) but was never knocked down in HCT116, so its share cannot be checked.

6 · How PPP1R15B fares in the benchmark

Ranking all 36.7 million training pairs and keeping the top 0.1% (|S| ≥ 3.93) puts PPP1R15B pairs in the top list, the 9th most of 3,806 training regulators. This one gene reproduces the project's main finding in miniature: choosing the regulator does most of the work (its pairs are hits about 40 times as often as a random pair), while ranking its targets helps only a little (within-regulator odds ratio 1.36; across held-out regulators, no detectable information at all).

34 of its 53 top-ranked hits (64%) have the predicted direction. Whether this beats chance is not established: the project's direction test fails its negative control (see Results).

7 · Optional: the context layer

The context layer pushes highly expressed HCT116 genes to the top. PPP1R15B's top ten become housekeeping-type genes (ribosomal proteins, actin, EEF1A1, a metallothionein), all among the 0.4% most expressed genes in HCT116, although their copy-number scores are only 2.5–6.6. More of them are hits (7 of 10 against 3 of 10), but what the layer adds is detectability rather than biology.

Limitations and when not to use it

Assumptions

  • Effects are linear on the log scale and shared across lineages; heterogeneity between lineages is not modelled.
  • The covariates and other arms' copy number capture the confounding. Tumour purity was not available; cell lines are assumed pure.
  • A's copy number reaches B only through A or through a gene in A's block (the instrument assumption). Attribution handles neighbours only partly.
  • DepMap release 26Q3 is inferred; file checksums could not be verified against the portal. Ploidy and whole-genome doubling come from an older release and are missing (imputed) for 30% of lines.

Not validated

  • Ranking targets within a regulator (tested; no information found).
  • Direction of effect (test miscalibrated).
  • Any cell line other than HCT116.
  • The value of attribution on held-out data (not ablated).
  • The claim that expression-factor correction would have hurt (taken from the literature).
  • Nothing has been checked by a human.

When not to use it

  • To pick targets for a regulator you care about. Within a regulator, the ranking is at chance.
  • For regulators that fail the cis gate. Their scores are 0 by construction.
  • To name the causal gene inside a large amplicon. Attribution is decided by a prior on cis strength, not by trans data.
  • As a list of significant pairs. The 5%-FDR call set has 10.7 million pairs, because genuine arm structure inflates the statistics (genomic inflation λ ≈ 2.1).
  • To predict the sign of a knockdown effect.

Precision

Only 14 held-out arms, so intervals are wide (1.33–11.2). Many secondary held-out metrics are reported without multiplicity correction; only the pre-registered primary metric should be read as confirmatory. Several components (the context layer and its controls) were added after seeing training results.

How we got here

The final method is what survived three filters: an association model chosen from the literature before any data were scored, two add-ons (the cis gate and attribution) that each passed a strict two-fold rule on training regulators, and controls that showed where every gain came from. The whole project ran in about 5½ hours on 27 October 2026, driven by one main AI agent (the "PI") and 19 sub-agents, with no human messages between the brief and the final report.

Why the method looks the way it does

  • It adjusts for other chromosomes' arms and never for expression factors because the literature says expression factors absorb copy-number effects and arm events are co-selected (TP1).
  • It is judged on HCT116 with arm-level hold-out and permutation tests because the data showed the HEK293T screen is dominated by artefacts and that hits cluster on a few regulators (TP2), and seven reviewer agents rebuilt the evaluation (TP3).
  • It has a cis gate and an attribution step because reviewers turned the PI's sketches into precise, pre-registered options (TP4), and these were the only two options that passed the adoption rule (TP6).
  • It is reported against hub baselines and within-regulator controls because the first numbers showed a label-free baseline beating the model 12-fold (TP5), and a diagnosis then found no detectable target-level information in the model (TP7).
  • The context layer is a separate output, not part of the method because a control that shuffles targets within each regulator kept its whole gain, so the gain came from target detectability, not from copy-number knowledge of specific pairs (TP7).
  • Its claims are narrow because the single held-out run was not significant on the headline test, and the direction test later failed its negative control (TP8).

TP118:21Use the reduced form, adjust for other chromosomes' arms, never for expression factorsLiterature on dosage-driven expression and co-selected arms set the core association model before any data were scored.

Situation and question

The brief asked for a copy-number→trans-expression model that accounts for cis copy number and covariates, and warned that correlated arm events can confound trans pairs. How to remove that confounding without removing real trans signal?

Options

  • Regress out expression principal components or PEER factors (the eQTL default).
  • Copy-number principal components.
  • Arm copy number of all arms except those on A's chromosome.
  • Also adjust A's own arm ("focal").
  • Generalised least squares with a copy-number kinship matrix; per-lineage meta-analysis.

Deciding evidence (literature)

  • Copy number correlates positively with mRNA for 99% of well-expressed genes, so top expression components are partly copy-number effects [F1].
  • Global expression correction removes spurious trans hotspots but also genuine ones [K1]; adjusting for phenotype PCs inflates false positives more than 10-fold [D2].
  • Recurrent arm events occur in more than 30% of tumours, with 88 co-occurring arm pairs, so cross-chromosome arm correlation is the main trans confounder [T1, S4, D3].
  • Reviewers added a leave-own-chromosome-out aneuploidy burden, TP53 status, RNA library type, and a deletion flag for B.

Outcome

The core model regresses B on A's copy number with arms outside A's chromosome adjusted, solved in closed form (matches a standard regression to 1e-8 on 480 real pairs). Later, three alternatives failed to beat it (focal +0.68 but unstable, leave-one-arm-out +0.04, copy-number PCs −0.02).

Caveat. Plain expression-PC correction was never run, so "PCs remove real signal" is believed, not shown here. The model on its own was weak (training OR 1.59, p 0.25).

What was learned

The project's main lesson is that copy-number associations, if they say anything here, say which regulators matter rather than which targets respond (weakly supported), and that a pooled enrichment score on a single-line Perturb-seq screen mostly rewards hubs and detectable targets. Insights are ranked within each category by how widely they apply, then by how surprising they are. not shown marks a belief this project did not demonstrate.

Scientific: the top two

  1. Which regulators, not which targets. Regulators whose own mRNA strongly tracks their copy number are more likely to have detectable trans effects on knockdown (gate: +0.97 log-OR on held-out arms, CI 0.30–1.64; the overall enrichment itself was not significant). Among a regulator's targets, copy number ranks responders at chance (AUROC 0.500). More
  2. A regulator's trans row is mostly its arm's signature. A gated regulator's t-statistics correlate with its chromosome arm's at median r = 0.87, so neighbours on an arm share nearly the same predictions. More

Methodological: the top two

  1. Split pooled enrichment into its parts before crediting a method: regulator choice, target detectability and pair-specific evidence. A +1.57 log-OR gain here contained no detectable pair-level knowledge. More
  2. Tune on a metric that measures the claim. Tuning on pooled odds ratio adopted only regulator-level gains and rejected the one option that improved target ranking. More

1 · Scientific and domain insights

  1. DepMap copy number carries no detectable information about which targets of a given regulator respond to its knockdown.

    Per-regulator AUROC 0.507 (training, 333 regulators) and 0.500 (test, 179); regulator-stratified odds ratio 0.94 on test (CI 0.77–1.15, so any lift is below ~15%); shuffling targets within each regulator: 3.76 vs 3.85. Adjusting A's own arm does no better (AUROC 0.511).

    Arose at TP7, confirmed at TP8

  2. A regulator's cis dosage response predicts whether its knockdown has detectable trans consequences, even though it is measured across all lineages rather than in the screened line.

    The gate (F ≥ 100) gave +0.79 log-OR on training, rising with the threshold, and +0.97 (CI 0.30–1.64) on test. Its target-level effect is −0.05: the gain is choosing regulators.

    Arose at TP4 and TP6; replicated at TP8

  3. A regulator's trans-association row is mostly the signature of its chromosome arm.

    Median r = 0.87 between a gated regulator's t-row and its arm-only row; 91% of rows exceed 0.7; 54 regulators on 1q share 1,308 top-ranked targets. Arm copy number alone does not enrich (training 0.97, test 0.61).

    Arose at TP7

  4. In a single-line CRISPRi Perturb-seq screen, regulator hubness and target expression depth largely decide which pairs count as hits.

    A label-free DepMap hub prior scores 19.7 (training) and 22.8 (test) against 5.00 and 3.85 for the copy-number method. Even the context fusion built on shuffled copy number (10.6) beats the copy-number method on test (−1.01 log-OR, CI −1.99 to −0.04).

    Arose at TP2 and TP5; quantified at TP7

  5. Whether a regulator is expressed in the screened line changes its hit yield about 20-fold, so a population-level instrument is not a context-level one.

    Among gated regulators, the per-pair hit rate rises from 0.0006 to 0.0124 across HCT116 expression bins of A. MYCN passes the gate through neuroblastoma amplicons, is not expressed in HCT116, and took 1,701 top-ranked training slots for 1 hit.

    Arose at TP2, TP7

  6. Whether copy number predicts the direction of a knockdown effect is not established. not established

    Test concordance 71.8% vs 64.7% under a within-regulator shuffle (p = 0.012, n = 131, only 6 regulators with ≥ 5 overlaps). The shuffled-copy-number fusion control passes the same test (58.5% vs 53.2%, p = 0.001), and the result is not significant in the 999-line set (p 0.13) or the least-leaky arms (p 0.48).

    Arose at TP8; corrected 28 Oct (decision note D9)

  7. Trans t-statistics skew negative, more so for highly expressed targets; part of this is a TPM compositional artefact.

    t ≤ −3 is twice as common as t ≥ 3 (3.5% vs 1.7%); mean t falls from −0.075 to −0.311 across target-expression quintiles. Renormalising TPM without A's chromosome moves the chr1 row mean from −0.12 to +0.09 but does not change the ranking (+0.05). The remainder is attributed to an own-chromosome aneuploidy response not shown.

    Arose at TP7

  8. HEK293T trans hits in X-Atlas/Orion are dominated by perturbations of non-expressed genes and are probably artefacts.

    83.0% of HEK293T trans hits against 12.7% in HCT116, with a different up/down balance (59% up). The copy-number method fails there (test OR 0.49, 6 overlaps). Artefact status is inferred not shown.

    Arose at TP2

  9. The copy-number signal is not centred on transcription factors. not shown: low power

    RNA-binding-protein regulators OR 3.56 (30 top-ranked hits); transcription factors 0 hits (137 regulators); ribosomal 0 hits in 631 top-ranked pairs. Displayed ORs for empty cells are correction artefacts.

    Arose at TP8

2 · Methodological insights that generalise

  1. Split any pooled enrichment into regulator allocation, target detectability and pair-specific evidence before crediting a method.

    The detectability fusion gained +1.57 log-OR and passed the strict adoption rule, but the exact within-regulator target shuffle gave the same odds ratio (24.5 vs 24.1). About 94% of the best model's log-enrichment was regulator allocation.

    Arose at TP5, TP7

  2. Choose the tuning objective from the scientific claim, not from the headline metric.

    Tuning on pooled odds ratio adopted only allocation gains (gate: target-level −0.05) and rejected the only option that improved target ranking (shrunk instrumental-variable score: +0.42 target-level, pooled lower bound below 0). A reviewer had proposed a target-level objective at round 1; both retrospectives list the choice as a mistake.

    Arose at TP3, TP6, TP7

  3. Give every control, metric and threshold a known-answer test before its first real use.

    A loader bug made the target-shuffle control run in an approximate mode and report a spurious pair-specific gain (+0.063, lower bound +0.002; exact value −0.02). A frozen effect-size filter assumed the wrong scale and kept 98.6% of positives. One adoption rule could not be met by construction.

    Arose at TP7 (and TP3)

  4. A within-regulator sign-shuffle test treats targets as exchangeable, so it can be anti-conservative when targets respond in correlated modules.

    The shuffled-copy-number fusion control, whose signs should carry no information, scored p = 0.001 (n = 354); the plain shuffled-copy-number control did not (p 0.52). The module explanation is an inference not tested. A calibrated test would need to permute regulators rather than target signs.

    Arose at TP8; correction D9

  5. Stratified "pair-specific" statistics must be stratified on the context that drives detectability.

    Stratifying by pan-DepMap expression deciles still let target expression alone, with no pair information, score 3.8 (training) and 4.1 (test). Stratifying by HCT116 expression reduced but did not remove this (3.4).

    Arose at TP5, TP7

  6. Hold out copy-number-derived predictions by chromosome arm, and budget for the precision this costs.

    Leakage: median highest test–training copy-number correlation 0.19 (none above 0.8), against 0.56–0.82 within 5–10 Mb. Cost: 14 test arms, CI 1.33–11.2, primary p = 0.068.

    Arose at TP3, TP8

  7. On hub-dominated benchmarks, require a gain in both folds plus a paired lower bound above zero.

    It rejected focal handling (+0.68, folds −0.09 / +1.52; not better on test) and coarser blocks; the gain it adopted (the gate) replicated on test.

    Arose at TP6, TP8

  8. Compute cheap label-free baselines before freezing a design that has to beat them.

    The hub prior was specified at round 1 but first computed after the seal (19.7 against 1.59); the frozen requirement to beat it was never met (−1.37 log-OR on training).

    Arose at TP5

  9. With near-collinear candidates (r ≥ 0.95), single-effect fine-mapping is decided by the prior, not the data.

    With σ₀ = 0.05 and about 1,335 degrees of freedom, log Bayes factor ≈ 0.385 z², a near-hard choice; attribution's within-regulator AUROC was 0.498. Predicted by the algorithms reviewer at round 1.

    Arose at TP4, TP6

  10. A credible single test needs physical sealing, a committed selection, a committed lock and a mock run on the same code path.

    Seal 57f2de3 → selection 62e67b9 → lock 603a7f5 → run e9085c7. The mock plus a fresh pre-final review found 3 fixes that would have been impossible after the run.

    Arose at TP3, TP8

  11. Every summary document needs an independent number-by-number check.

    The verifier checked about 120 numbers and found 4 errors and 3 over-claims, including a Fisher p of 2×10⁻³⁶ shown next to the pre-registered p of 0.068. The direction over-claim survived until a later control check.

    Arose at TP8

3 · Engineering details for reproduction

  1. Core engine: one pivoted-QR residualisation per regulator chromosome, then closed-form partial correlations.

    23 float64 residualisations plus matrix products score 2.2×10⁸ pairs in 67 s on a 10-core laptop; matches standard OLS to 1e-8 on 480 real pairs.

    Arose at TP1

  2. Guard missing values inside the Bayes-factor step.

    Genes with copy number but no expression produced a missing degrees-of-freedom value that propagated through the log-sum-exp and wiped out α for 28.5% of pairs; fixed by setting their co-expression z to 0.

    Arose at TP6

  3. At the final gate setting, failing regulators can be zeroed or down-weighted with identical results.

    Weights 0 and 0.5 give the same top 0.1%, because gated regulators fill it. 5,403 of 12,141 pass.

    Arose at TP6

  4. Use only the sign and p-value of the PIE differential-expression tables.

    Fold-change magnitudes are pseudocount artefacts (perturbed mean exactly 0 for 83.5% of expressed targets). The effect statistic is a signed rank-biserial in [−1, 1]; |value| ≥ 0.2 keeps 47% of positives.

    Arose at TP2, TP3

  5. Sample set and covariates.

    Use the 1,436-line set; impute missing ploidy/doubling (30% of lines) with an indicator; drop sequencing platform (93% missing); TP53 from older mutation matrices covers 1,255 of 1,436 lines. Sex-centre X copy number before the variable-copy-number rule (male X sits at log2 ≈ −1).

    Arose at TP3

  6. Resampling conventions.

    Fix the top-K threshold at its full-split value in every replicate; permute regulator labels only within the same split and line; with 200 permutations p cannot go below 0.005 (the final run used 1,000). Seeds [20261027, 1, 2, 3, 4], streams spawned from 20261027.

    Arose at TP3

  7. Compute fusion percentiles over all DepMap-measured targets, never within the benchmark universe.

    Universe membership is derived from benchmark features, so percentiles inside it would leak. Fused score files must store the pair term and the target weight separately so the target-shuffle control can be exact.

    Arose at TP7

  8. Rank rather than call.

    Genomic inflation λ ≈ 2.1 on real data reflects genuine arm structure (0.9–1.12 under a simulated null); the resulting 5%-FDR call set (10.7 M pairs) has test OR 1.15.

    Arose at TP6, TP8

  9. Report odds ratios with empty cells as not available.

    Haldane-corrected values of 6.7 and 42.9 for classes with 0 hits are artefacts. A target-agnostic score degenerates under top-K.

    Arose at TP8

  10. Platform quirks.

    macOS Accelerate emits spurious matrix-multiply warnings (never promote them to errors). In this agent harness, sub-agents could not write report files, so the main agent saved condensed versions of their replies. The DepMap release could not be checksum-verified behind the portal's bot wall.

    Arose at TP1, TP2, TP8
