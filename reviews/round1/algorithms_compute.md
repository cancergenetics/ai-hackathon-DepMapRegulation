# Round 1 review: algorithms and compute

Reviewer: algorithms / scientific computing. Micro-benchmarks were run on this machine (M4, 10 cores, Accelerate BLAS, numpy/scipy from .venv) and on the real `depmap_cn_matched.parquet`; scripts are in the session scratchpad, not the repo.

## Verdict

The §5.1 plan (per-chromosome QR residualisation, then three residual correlation blocks, then closed-form first-order partial correlation) is correct and cheap. Compute is not the constraint here: a full-genome M1 pass is about 1 minute. Effort should go to correctness guards, evaluation machinery and richer variants. §10's "minutes" is a safe overstatement.

## Measured numbers

- **GEMM** (1037 × 1000 regulators against 18,000 targets): float64 0.09 s (about 400 GFLOP/s, using the Apple matrix unit through Accelerate), float32 0.02 s. A full 13k-regulator pass needs about 1.2 s (f64) per big GEMM, and M1 needs two of them (x_A with y_B, x_A with x_B). QR of Z (p ≈ 90) takes 8 ms, and residualising 18k columns 40 ms. Doing this for all 22 chromosomes is under 5 s.
- **p-values:** `scipy.special.stdtr` takes about 9 s per 1.4×10^8 pairs, `stats.t.sf` is about 3× slower. The elementwise partial-correlation formula costs about 70 ms per 1000 × 18,000 block, so about 2 s per genome.
- **Estimated wall clock per full M1 pass (S1, about 2×10^8 trans pairs, i.e. 13k autosomal regulators × 17k targets):** about 40–60 s including p-values. Without p-values (rank by |t| only) about 20 s. S2 (n = 1,509) is about 1.5× that.
- **Memory:** the largest block is chr1 (about 1,940 genes) × 18k × 8 B = 280 MB per matrix; with about 6 temporaries that is about 2 GB peak. A dense float32 matrix of t for all regulators × targets is 13k × 18k × 4 B = 0.94 GB (β adds another 0.94 GB), so it fits on disk or in RAM. The "sparse plus float16" storage in §10 is unnecessary and harmful (see 5).
- **Permutations:** about 20 s per null pass (without p-values), so 100 full-genome null passes take about 35 min and 1,000 take about 5.5 h. That is affordable for the final method only.
- **GLS:** one 1037³ eigendecomposition takes under 1 s, so 22 leave-one-chromosome-out (LOCO) kernels are trivial (see 9).
- **Total for roughly 50 tuning variants plus the final run:** about 1–3 h of compute.

## Numerical findings from the real data

- Within a chromosome, adjacent-gene log2 CN correlation has median 0.97 (1st percentile 0.76). Genes in a DepMap "segment" are not bit-identical (adjacent columns differ in all but 2 of 18,279 cases), so segments cannot be collapsed by exact hashing.
- Breakpoint segmentation at adjacent r < t gives 1,251 / 4,446 / 11,902 / 16,317 segments for t = 0.90 / 0.95 / 0.98 / 0.99. The result is very sensitive to the threshold.
- R² of an autosomal gene's log2 CN on the 40 arm medians is median 0.56. Fewer than 1% of genes exceed 0.9 and none exceed 0.99, so collinearity of x_B with arm CN is mild and arm collinearity is not a numerical threat. cond(Z) = 8 for intercept plus arms, so QR is not required for stability at this p; float32 normal equations gave max error 2×10^-6 in r.
- numpy here uses Accelerate. Matmuls emit spurious "divide by zero / overflow / invalid value encountered in matmul" RuntimeWarnings while results are correct; I checked them against float64 references. Results are bitwise identical at 1, 4 and 10 threads, and `VECLIB_MAXIMUM_THREADS` has no visible effect.

## Numbered changes

1. **MUST: use float64 for residualisation and the partial-correlation algebra, with QR (or SVD with rcond) rather than normal equations.** The cost is negligible (1.2 s per GEMM). Cast to float32 only for storage. Compute p-values from t in float64 using `log` of the survival function. `stdtr` underflows to 0 at |t| ≳ 55, so ranking must use |t| or z, never p. Assert `cond(Z) < 1e6` per chromosome and fail loudly; this matters for S2 with missingness indicators, lineage dummies and merged media levels, and for the stratified variant (13).
2. **MUST: guard the recursion.** Flag pairs as NaN where 1 − r_AB² or 1 − r_BY² < 1e-3, or where the residual SD of x_A or x_B is below 1% of its raw SD (x_B almost fully explained by arms). Use first-order partial correlation with df = n − p − 2 (p counts the intercept). Constant targets after residualisation must be dropped explicitly. Never let any code path turn warnings into errors, because of the spurious Accelerate matmul warnings; check outputs with `np.isfinite` instead.
3. **MUST: a generic "pair-specific regressor" engine, not a one-off formula.** M2 (cis gating), M3a (mediation with y_A) and joint (x_A, y_A) models all reduce to: given the k × k correlation block among {pair-specific variables} after residualising Z, compute the t of one coefficient. Implement this as a batched Gaussian-elimination sweep with closed-form 2×2 and 3×3 cases (k ≤ 4 is just four big GEMMs plus elementwise algebra), about 3–5 min per genome. Test the engine against `statsmodels.OLS` on random data to 1e-8 relative for the t-statistic, including a case with k = 3 and a rank-deficient case. This is the single most valuable unit test.
4. **MUST: do not materialise labels or a dense universe mask.** The universe is rank-1 (the cell line's target-column set) minus chromosome blocks, so N = Σ_A (n_targets − n_targets_on_chr(A)) in O(G). Pre-filter the 102M-row DE table once to a small positives table (about 224k trans hits at p_adj < 0.05, int32 regulator and target indices plus sign), and cache it. Key positives as `a*G_t + b` in int64 and test top-K membership with `np.isin` against a sorted key array.
5. **MUST: store, per regulator, a score histogram per label class (benchmark-negative, positive-concordant, positive-discordant) over about 2,048 |t| bins** (log-spaced in the tail), in addition to the dense float32 t and β matrices of the final method. This is a sufficient statistic for every metric in §6: the global Fisher OR and p at any K (thresholds from cumulative sums), the per-regulator Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel strata, the sign-concordant OR, and 1,000 bootstrap replicates (multinomial weights over 5 Mb bins as a (1000 × R) @ (R × 2048) product, taking seconds). The array is 9k × 2048 × 3 × 4 B ≈ 220 MB per variant. Ties: never use float16 (about 3 digits, so ties at the top). Break ties deterministically in a seeded way and log it.
6. **SHOULD: fix what the bootstrap and Fisher p mean.** Resampling regulator bins leaves the target dependence intact, and the effective number of independent regulators is about 4–5k segments, not 9k. Report Fisher p as descriptive and rely on the bin bootstrap plus the NC1/B0 controls for inference. Also report top-K at fixed K = 0.1% of the universe and at fixed absolute K (e.g. 10^4, 10^5), because 0.1% of 26–62M pairs is 26–62k pairs and is dominated by a few hubs. Include the CMH OR and a "top-n per regulator" OR.
7. **SHOULD: add a focal variant (no per-chromosome loop).** Residualise once on [Z, all arms], including A's own arm, so x_A is within-arm CN. Cost is one global QR plus one pass. This tests whether M1 signal is arm-level (about 400 genes sharing the t) or focal. Consider also 41 per-arm groups, excluding only A's own arm and keeping the other arm of the chromosome; it is compute-neutral. With the current LOCO design, every gene on an arm gets nearly the same t, so top-K will be dominated by a handful of arm effects and the Fisher OR is then an arm-level OR with a huge effective-n inflation. The reviewers on statistics and evaluation should weigh this.
8. **SHOULD: segment collapsing algorithm.** Do greedy sequential assignment along the chromosome. A gene joins the current segment if its correlation with the segment mean profile (computed on residualised x) exceeds the threshold. Do not use adjacent-link (single-linkage) breakpoints, which chain. Cost is a banded window (50 genes) × n, so under a second. Expect about 4.4k segments at 0.95 (a 4× reduction in tests); freeze the threshold before tuning and report the sensitivity at 0.90 / 0.98. Collapsing is a compute and test-count device as much as a statistical one; segment-level BH is more honest than gene-level BH.
9. **SHOULD: SuSiE-like attribution should be single-effect regression (L = 1) with a closed-form Wakefield ABF, not iterative IBSS.** For a locus of m genes and target B, PIP_j ∝ π_j · ABF(z_j). Vectorise over m × 15k targets, with no iterations. Co-segmented genes have r ≈ 0.97–1.0, so CN-only z-scores cannot discriminate between them. Discrimination must come from the prior π_j (cis strength t_cis, expression level, optionally mediation z). State this explicitly; otherwise SuSiE will return near-uniform PIPs and add nothing. Multi-effect (L ≥ 2) is not identifiable here; drop it.
10. **SHOULD: GLS/LMM design.** A per-target variance ratio δ_B makes the whitening target-specific, so it cannot be factorised across pairs. Estimate δ per target under the null, quantise to about 8 bins, and whiten X, Y and Z once per bin (8 passes, about 5–8 min). Use LOCO kernels (22 eigendecompositions) built from CN of chromosomes ≠ c, matching the per-chromosome loop and avoiding proximal contamination. Because the arms are already covariates, check that K is not redundant (compare its rank structure to the arm design); the realistic benefit is small.
11. **SHOULD: permutation controls.** Permute the rows of (X, arm CN) jointly within lineage, leaving Y and Z fixed. Permuting only X leaves the arm covariates inconsistent with x_A, and this tests something different. Run 20–100 passes, accumulating only a pooled histogram of |t|; this gives an empirical FDR at about 1.4×10^10 null values for about 35 min. Report genomic-inflation λ (median z² / 0.455) per regulator chromosome on the real data as well.
12. **SHOULD: handle targets without CN.** 659 expressed genes are absent from the CN matrix and 13 MT genes have all-NaN CN. Either drop them from the universe (document the count and effect on the Perturb-seq-measured set) or run a "no x_B" variant for them. Decide explicitly; do not let them silently become NaN rows.
13. **SHOULD: lineage-stratified meta-analysis is under-determined with the full covariate set.** Lineages have n = 20–180 against p ≈ 40 arms plus Z, so per-lineage df are 0–100. Use ridge-shrunk residualisation or top arm PCs within lineage; cost is the same total flops. Otherwise restrict it to lineages with n ≥ 100.
14. **COULD: rank / inverse-normal transform of Y** (per-column argsort, about 1 s) with explicit tie handling, since expressed genes have zero-heavy columns. Also consider closed-form HC0 robust variance: it costs about 6 extra GEMMs (Σ x²y², Σ x³y, …), which is affordable and addresses leverage from rare focal amplifications (the "≥ 20 lines with |log2| ≥ 1" regulators have about 20 informative samples, so t-asymptotics are poor).
15. **COULD: add RNA stranded/unstranded (979/745 lines) as a technical covariate**, since it is available and affects y_A–y_B co-expression strongly (important for M3a mediation). This is for the data reviewer to confirm.

## Software structure and testing (extends §9)

- **Layout:** add `config.py` (seeds, paths, thresholds in one frozen dataclass written to every output's metadata), `design.py` (Z builders with the `cond` check), `engine.py` (item 3, pure functions on arrays, no IO), `evalhist.py` (items 4–5), `cache/` for residualised matrices keyed by a hash of (sample set, covariates, chromosome). Index conventions: regulators and targets sorted by (chrom, start) in canonical arrays. Chromosome blocks are then contiguous slices, and cis masking is a slice.
- **Chunking rule:** one regulator chromosome at a time, further split into regulator blocks of 500 for chr1/2 (peak memory under 3 GB). Run single-process with BLAS-level parallelism. AMX is shared per cluster, so multiprocess pools will not scale, and they oversubscribe cores with the pyarrow/polars thread pools. Log `np.show_config()` and the numpy version with every run; use tolerance-based (1e-9) not bitwise regression tests.
- **Tests, known-answer:** (a) engine vs `statsmodels` (item 3); (b) simulation with planted trans effect, arm confounder and lineage confounder, recovering β and a sign convention test; (c) FWL identity: the t from per-chromosome closed form equals the full OLS t with all covariates for 50 random pairs of real data; (d) the histogram metric equals a brute-force dense Fisher on a 200 × 2,000 toy; (e) permutation null has λ ≈ 1; (f) split-guard test: the tuning API raises on TEST labels. Make the guard structural: write TEST labels to a separate file that only the `--final` script path can load; a runtime flag alone is easy to bypass.

## No change needed

- Per-chromosome residualisation as such, and exclusion of the regulator's chromosome arms (statistically their problem, computationally fine).
- The sample set and gene counts: all dense computations fit comfortably in 32 GB and a single process.
- The overall chunking idea in §10 (the numbers are better than stated).
- Dropping expression PCs from the covariate set: no computational objection.
